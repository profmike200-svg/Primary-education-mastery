# Índices mejorados de los 25 temas de Inglés

> Propuesta de índices reorganizados a partir de los temas aportados por el docente (títulos oficiales confirmados en [[CLM_Mapa_Temario_Ingles]]). No reproduce el texto de los temas. Los autores citados son referencias habituales en cada campo: **comprobar cada cita antes de usarla en el examen**.

## Criterios de mejora aplicados a todos los temas
1. **Cada epígrafe del título oficial tiene un apartado propio** (el tribunal comprueba que se desarrolla todo el título).
2. Dentro de cada apartado: **teoría (autores) → aplicación al aula de Primaria → ejemplo concreto**.
3. Un apartado explícito de **atención a la diversidad** (Decreto 85/2018, DUA) en los temas didácticos.
4. **Evidencia actual** cuando existe (metaanálisis, guías): diferencia frente a temarios de academia.
5. **Normativa vigente** (LOMLOE, RD 157/2022, Decreto 81/2022, Orden 185/2022, Decreto 85/2018) aplicada, no solo citada.
6. Introducción con gancho y relevancia; conclusión que vuelva al gancho y sintetice; bibliografía y webgrafía al final.

Estructura tipo: **1. Introduction · 2-5. Development (uno por epígrafe del título) · 6. Conclusion · 7. Legal references, bibliography and web resources.**

## Bloque I · Language and communication

### Topic 1 · Language as communication. Spoken and written language. Factors defining a linguistic situation
1. Introduction: communication in daily life and at school; LOMLOE key competences (CCL, CP).
2. Language as communication
   2.1. Definitions of language (Bloch & Trager, Crystal) and of communication
   2.2. Human language vs. other communication systems
3. Spoken language and written language
   3.1. Oral pre-eminence (historical, ontogenetic and curricular reasons)
   3.2. Differences between oral and written language (Crystal)
   3.3. Classroom implications: oral first, written as support; UDL
4. Functionality of language
   4.1. Jakobson's six functions (with classroom examples)
   4.2. Halliday's functions in child language
5. Factors defining a linguistic situation
   5.1. Elements of communication: sender, receiver, message, channel, code, context
   5.2. Context and communicative competence (Hymes' SPEAKING model)
   5.3. Classroom application: creating real communicative situations
6. Conclusion · 7. References
- **Biblioteca**: [[Expresion_Oral]] · [[Comprension_Oral_L2]] · [[Linea_Desarrollo_Lenguaje]]
- **Cambios**: se añade Halliday (desarrollo infantil del lenguaje) y un subapartado de aplicación en los puntos 3 y 5.

### Topic 2 · Communication in the FL classroom: verbal and non-verbal communication. Extralinguistic strategies
1. Introduction
2. Communication in the foreign language classroom
   2.1. The classroom as a communicative context: input, interaction, output
   2.2. The communicative approach (brief; developed in topics 13-14)
3. Verbal communication in the English classroom
   3.1. Classroom language and teacher talk; English as the main language of the classroom
   3.2. Principles: low-anxiety environment (affective filter, Krashen), integrated skills, real purposes (eTwinning)
4. Non-verbal communication
   4.1. Concept and classifications (Knapp; kinesics, proxemics, paralanguage)
   4.2. A note of caution: Mehrabian's percentages apply only to the communication of feelings and attitudes, not to all communication
5. Extralinguistic strategies: non-verbal reactions to messages in different contexts
   5.1. TPR and physical responses (Asher)
   5.2. Gestures, mime, visual support, realia
   5.3. Reading pupils' non-verbal feedback to check understanding
6. Attention to diversity: TEA (explicit teaching of non-verbal cues), TDL, newly arrived pupils
7. Conclusion · 8. References
- **Biblioteca**: [[Respuesta_Fisica_Total_TPR]] · [[Uso_de_la_Lengua_Materna_en_Clase_de_Ingles]] · [[TEA]] · [[Expresion_e_Interaccion_Oral_L2]]

### Topic 9 · The phonological system of English. Models and techniques. Perception, discrimination, rhythm and stress. Phonetic correction

   3.1. Vowels (monophthongs, diphthongs) and consonants
   3.2. Suprasegmental features: word stress, sentence stress, rhythm (stress-timed), intonation, weak forms, connected speech
   3.3. Contrastive analysis with Spanish
4. Perception and discrimination of sounds
5. Rhythm and stress in the classroom (chants, songs, clapping)
6. Models and techniques of learning
   6.1. Models: imitation, analytic-linguistic, integrative

7. Phonetic correction: priorities (intelligibility), techniques, error types
8. Conclusion · 9. References
- **Biblioteca**: [[Pronunciacion_Ingles]] · [[CLM_Supuesto_02_Fonetica]] · [[Lee_2015_Pronunciacion]] · [[Derwing_2005_Inteligibilidad]]
