# Topic 9 · Development script

> 📘 **Texto completo del tema: [[CLM_Tema_09]]**

**Title**: Description of the phonological system of the English language. Models and techniques of learning. Perception, discrimination, rhythms and stresses. The phonetic correction.

> Guion para redactar el tema. Comprueba antes de citar las referencias marcadas como *clásico*, y verifica las transcripciones con un diccionario de pronunciación. Ver [[CLM_Como_Escribir_un_Tema_de_10]] y [[Pronunciacion_Ingles]].

## 1. Introduction
- **Hook (option)**: *"'I want to buy a sheet', said a pupil in the shop role-play, and the whole class laughed. One sound had changed the meaning — and the mood — of the conversation. Pronunciation is not a decoration of language: it is what makes us understood."*
- **Relevance**: intelligibility is the goal (Derwing & Munro, 2005 — ✔), and explicit pronunciation teaching works (Lee, Jang & Plonsky, 2015, meta-analysis — ✔). The curriculum includes sounds, rhythm and intonation within the basic knowledge of the Communication block.
- **Outline**.

## 2. Phonetics and phonology: key concepts
- **Phonetics** (physical production and perception of sounds) vs **phonology** (the sound system of a language and how sounds distinguish meaning).
- **Phoneme**, **allophone**, **minimal pair** (*ship/sheep*); segmental vs suprasegmental features.

## 3. Description of the English phonological system
- **3.1 Segmental features** (standard British English / RP as a reference model, as described by Roach — *clásico*): **44 phonemes** in the usual description: **20 vowels** (12 monophthongs, 8 diphthongs) and **24 consonants**. Vowels classified by tongue height, tongue position and lip rounding; consonants by place, manner and voicing.
- **3.2 Suprasegmental features**: **word stress** (e.g. *PHOtograph, phoTOgrapher*), **sentence stress** on content words, **rhythm** (English traditionally described as stress-timed and Spanish as syllable-timed; the dichotomy is useful in class but debated in research), **weak forms** and the **schwa** /ə/, **connected speech** (linking, elision), **intonation** (falling and rising patterns).
- **3.3 Contrastive analysis with Spanish** (key for teaching in Spain): 5 Spanish vowels vs about 12 English monophthongs (vowel length and quality: /ɪ/–/iː/); no schwa in Spanish; /ʃ/, /z/, /v/–/b/, /h/ vs /x/; initial /s/ + consonant (*eschool*); final consonant clusters and *-ed/-s* endings; stress and rhythm.

## 4. Perception, discrimination, rhythms and stresses
- **Perception before production**: children need to hear a contrast before they can produce it.
- **Discrimination activities**: minimal-pair games (stand up/sit down, pictures, bingo), odd one out, sound categorisation.
- **Rhythm and stress activities**: clapping syllables and stress, chants (Graham's *Jazz Chants* — *clásico*), songs and rhymes, stress dominoes, backchaining long sentences.
- **Example (5th year)**: *-ed detectives*: pupils sort past forms by sound (/t/, /d/, /ɪd/) and discover the rule.

## 5. Models and techniques of learning
- **Approaches** (Celce-Murcia, Brinton & Goodwin — *clásico*): **intuitive-imitative** (listening and imitating models: dominant with young children) and **analytic-linguistic** (explicit information: articulation diagrams, phonemic symbols, rules: more useful from the third cycle); an **integrative** approach combines both within communicative tasks.
- **Which model of English?** A standard reference model (British or American) for teaching, exposure to other accents, and **intelligibility** as the goal; Jenkins' *Lingua Franca Core* (2000 — *clásico*) prioritises the features most important for international intelligibility.
- **Techniques by cycle**: 1st cycle: songs, rhymes, TPR, imitation games; 2nd cycle: minimal pairs, phonics links with spelling, chants; 3rd cycle: phonemic symbols for key sounds, stress marking, recordings and self-assessment, AI-assisted pronunciation tools with critical use.

## 6. The phonetic correction
- **Priorities**: correct what affects **intelligibility** first (vowel length, word stress, final consonants) rather than every accent feature (Derwing & Munro, 2005 — ✔).
- **Techniques**: modelling and recasts, prompts for self-correction, mirrors and gestures for articulation (e.g. hand under the chin, fingers for vowel length), minimal-pair contrasts, peer feedback, recordings.
- **Climate**: correction must not cause anxiety; correct individually or chorally, never ridicule (link with [[CLM_Supuesto_07_Conflicto_y_Convivencia]]).

## 7. Attention to diversity
- Pupils with hearing difficulties (visual cues, position, captions), TDL (more modelling and repetition), dyslexia (phonological awareness work, oral priority) ([[Discapacidad_Auditiva]], [[TDL]], [[Dislexia]]).

## 8. Conclusion
- **Model idea**: *"Back to the 'sheet' that should have been a 'sheep': our pupils do not need to sound like native speakers, but they do need to be understood. A good pronunciation programme starts with listening, moves through discrimination and playful practice, and treats correction as help, not as judgement."*
