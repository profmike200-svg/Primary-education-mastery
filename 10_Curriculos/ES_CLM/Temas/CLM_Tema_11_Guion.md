# Topic 11 · Development script

**Title**: Lexical and semantic fields of English language. Lexicon required for socialization, information and expression of attitudes. Types of activities related to the teaching-learning of vocabulary in the foreign language class.

> Guion para redactar el tema. Comprueba antes de citar las referencias marcadas como *clásico*. Ver [[CLM_Como_Escribir_un_Tema_de_10]] y [[Vocabulario_L2]].

## 1. Introduction
- **Hook (option)**: *"A ten-year-old can survive in an English-speaking playground with very little grammar, but not without words: 'Can I play?', 'My turn!', 'Cool!'. As Wilkins put it, without grammar very little can be conveyed; without vocabulary nothing can be conveyed."* (Wilkins, 1972 — *clásico*: comprueba la cita exacta antes de usarla entre comillas.)
- **Relevance**: vocabulary is the basis of comprehension and production; it appears across the Communication block of the curriculum.
- **Outline**.

## 2. General considerations about teaching words
- **What knowing a word means**: **form** (spoken, written, word parts), **meaning** (form-meaning link, concept, associations) and **use** (grammar, collocations, register) (Nation, 2013 — ✔ [[Nation_2013_Vocabulario_L2]]).
- **Frequency**: prioritise high-frequency words; topic lists alone are not enough.
- **Coverage**: comfortable reading requires knowing around **98 %** of the words in a text (Nation, 2013 — ✔).
- **Lexical chunks**: language is learned in multi-word units (*Can I…?*, *What's your favourite…?*) — Lewis' *Lexical Approach* (1993 — *clásico*).
- **Memory**: words are learned through many meaningful encounters, spaced over time, and through active retrieval (Dunlosky et al., 2013 — ✔ [[Practica_Espaciada]], [[Practica_de_Recuperacion]]).
- **Nation's four strands**: meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, language-focused learning and fluency development, with roughly equal time ✔.

## 3. Lexical and semantic fields of English
- **3.1 Lexical fields**: groups of words related by form or topic (word families: *teach, teacher, teaching*; topic-based sets: food, clothes, animals).
- **3.2 Semantic fields** (Trier's field theory — *clásico*): words that share a domain of meaning and define each other; **sense relations** (Lyons — *clásico*): synonymy (*big/large*), antonymy (*hot/cold*), hyponymy (*animal → dog, cat*), meronymy (*hand → finger*).
- **Classroom use**: mind maps, word webs, classification games; morphology (prefixes *un-*, suffixes *-er*, compounds *football*).

## 4. Lexicon for socialization, information and expression of attitudes
| Function | Examples (chunks) |
|---|---|
| **Socialization** | greetings and farewells, introductions (*This is…*), thanking and apologising, classroom language (*Can I go to the toilet, please?*) |
| **Information** | asking and giving personal information, time, location, descriptions (*Where is…? It's next to…*) |
| **Expression of attitudes** | likes and dislikes (*I love / I don't like*), feelings (*I'm tired / happy*), opinions (*I think… because…*), agreement (*Me too!*) |
Teach these as functional chunks from the first cycle and expand them progressively.

## 5. Types of vocabulary activities
- **5.1 Presentation**: realia, flashcards, mime and TPR, stories and songs, pictures; meaning before form, oral before written.
- **5.2 Practice and retrieval**: memory games, *Kim's game*, bingo, guessing games, spelling games, quizzes; spaced review in later units.
- **5.3 Consolidation and use**: tasks where words are needed (surveys, descriptions, projects), word walls, personal picture dictionaries, digital flashcards; extensive reading with graded readers.
- **Example (3rd year, food)**: real food in a bag (presentation) → *What's missing?* (retrieval) → class survey of favourite food (use) → healthy-lunchbox project (production) → quick retrieval quiz two weeks later (spacing).

## 6. Attention to diversity and assessment
- UDL: images and sounds for every word; fewer words with more repetitions for pupils with learning difficulties; picture dictionary for newly arrived pupils; extension (synonyms, word families) for high ability ([[Diseno_Universal_para_el_Aprendizaje]]).
- Assessment of vocabulary in use (not only lists): matching, picture tasks, oral and written production, self-assessment.

## 7. Conclusion
- **Model idea**: *"Words are the bricks of communication. Selecting the most useful ones, organising them in fields and functions, and meeting them again and again in meaningful, playful activities is what turns a list into real vocabulary our pupils can use."*
