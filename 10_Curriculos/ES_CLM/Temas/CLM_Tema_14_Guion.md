# Topic 14 · Development script

> 📘 **Texto completo del tema: [[CLM_Tema_14]]**

**Title**: Methods and techniques focused on the acquisition of communicative competence. Specific methodological basis for English language teaching.

> Guion para redactar el tema. Diferéncialo del tema 13: aquí lo central son las **técnicas actuales y los principios para Primaria**, no la historia de los métodos. Comprueba antes de citar las referencias marcadas como *clásico*. Ver [[CLM_Como_Escribir_un_Tema_de_10]].

## 1. Introduction
- **Hook (option)**: *"Two classrooms, same unit on animals. In one, pupils copy a list of words and a grammar rule. In the other, they plan a visit to the zoo, ask each other what animals they want to see and design a map in English. Both teachers are 'teaching English', but only one is teaching pupils to communicate."*
- **Relevance**: LOMLOE and RD 157/2022 define the FL area through communicative specific competences and learning situations; the methodology must serve that goal.
- **Outline**.

## 2. Approach, method and technique
- **Anthony (1963)** (*clásico*): **approach** (theory of language and learning) → **method** (overall plan) → **technique** (specific classroom activity).
- **Richards & Rodgers** (*clásico*): approach, design and procedure.
- Today: the **post-method** view (Kumaravadivelu, 2006 — *clásico*): no single best method; teachers apply principles to their context ("principled eclecticism").

## 3. Communicative competence (brief link to topic 3)
- Hymes (1972); Canale & Swain (1980); CEFR (2001, 2020): reception, production, interaction, mediation. The six LOMLOE specific competences are its curricular expression.

## 4. Methods and techniques focused on communicative competence
- **4.1 Communicative Language Teaching**: meaning first, real purposes, information gaps, fluency and accuracy.
- **4.2 Task-based learning** (Willis, 1996; Ellis, 2003 — *clásicos*): pre-task → task cycle (task, planning, report) → language focus.
- **4.3 Focus on form within communication**: explicit attention to grammar and vocabulary inside meaningful tasks; explicit instruction is more effective than implicit (Norris & Ortega, 2000, meta-analysis — ✔ [[Norris_2000_Instruccion_L2]]).
- **4.4 CLIL** (Coyle, Hood & Marsh, 2010 — *clásico*): the 4Cs (content, communication, cognition, culture); positive for language, evidence for content still limited ([[AICLE_CLIL]]).
- **4.5 Techniques especially suited to children**: **TPR** (Asher, 1969 — ✔, evidence mainly from the author's own studies, [[Respuesta_Fisica_Total_TPR]]); **storytelling** ([[Rutina_Cuentacuentos_en_Ingles]]); **songs and chants**; **games**; **drama and role-play**; **cooperative learning** with roles and individual accountability (Johnson & Johnson, 2009 — ✔ [[Aprendizaje_Cooperativo]]); **project-based learning**.
- **4.6 Digital tools and AI**: as support for input, practice and feedback, with purposeful and moderate use.

## 5. Specific methodological basis for English teaching in Primary
- **5.1 Curricular principles**: competence-based learning through **learning situations**; UDL; integration of skills; oral priority in the first cycle; assessment as part of learning (RD 157/2022; Decreto 81/2022; Orden 185/2022; Decreto 85/2018).
- **5.2 Principles from research with young learners** (Cameron, 2001; Pinter, 2017 — ✔):
  1. Children learn through **meaning and activity**, supported by context, routines and visuals.
  2. Lots of **comprehensible input** before demanding output; English as the main language of the classroom.
  3. **Scaffolding** that is gradually removed (Vygotsky's ZPD, Bruner) ([[Andamiaje]]).
  4. **Recycling and spaced review** of language ([[Practica_Espaciada]]).
  5. **Explicit focus on form** inside communication (Norris & Ortega, 2000).
  6. **Positive affective climate**: low anxiety, success, motivation ([[Autoeficacia]]).
  7. **Age-appropriate tasks** according to cognitive development ([[Etapa_6_8_anos]], [[Etapa_10_12_anos]]).
- **5.3 Example of a learning situation**: *"Our Town Guide for Visitors"* (6th year) combining CLT, task-based learning, cooperative work, mediation and digital tools ([[SA_Ingles_3er_Ciclo_Town_Guide]]).

## 6. Attention to diversity
- UDL; flexible grouping; adapted techniques for ADHD (movement, short tasks), dyslexia (oral priority), newly arrived pupils (TPR, visuals), high ability (open tasks).

## 7. Conclusion
- **Model idea**: *"There is no magic method. What research and the curriculum tell us is a set of principles: rich input, real communication, explicit attention to form, scaffolding, recycling and a safe classroom. Choosing the right technique for each moment and each child is the teacher's professional skill."*
