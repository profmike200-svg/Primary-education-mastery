# Topic 3 · Development script

**Title**: Developing linguistic skills: listening comprehension and expression, written comprehension and expression. Communicative competence in English.

> Guion para redactar el tema: ideas clave, autores y ejemplos por apartado. No es un texto para memorizar palabra por palabra: escríbelo con tu voz. Antes de citar, comprueba las referencias marcadas como *clásico*. Ver [[CLM_Como_Escribir_un_Tema_de_10]].

## 1. Introduction
- **Hook (option)**: *"Monday morning, 2nd year. Before saying a word, Lucía points at the window and shouts 'Rain!'. She has understood the weather song we sang on Friday, recognised the word and used it to communicate. In a few seconds she has listened, understood and spoken: that is what this topic is about."*
- **Relevance**: the aim of the FL area is not to know *about* English but to *communicate* in English. LOMLOE sets the development of **communicative competence** as the core of the area.
- **Curriculum link**: RD 157/2022 and Decreto 81/2022 organise the FL area into six specific competences (comprehension, production, interaction, mediation, plurilingualism, interculturality) and three blocks of basic knowledge (Communication, Plurilingualism, Interculturality); key competences CCL and CP.
- **Outline**: present the sections.

## 2. Linguistic skills
- **2.1 Classification**: receptive (listening, reading) and productive (speaking, writing); oral and written medium. Present Harmer's two-by-two grid in one sentence (Harmer, *The Practice of English Language Teaching* — *clásico*).
- **2.2 Integration and the CEFR view**: skills are rarely used in isolation (we read an email and reply; we listen and take notes). The CEFR (Council of Europe, 2001) and its *Companion Volume* (2020) move from "four skills" to **four modes of communication: reception, production, interaction and mediation** ([[Consejo_Europa_2020_MCER]]). This is the model behind the LOMLOE specific competences.
- **Classroom idea**: an integrated sequence in which pupils listen to a story, retell it orally, read a short version and write a new page.

## 3. Oral comprehension and expression
- **3.1 Listening**: the first skill to develop in Primary (natural order of acquisition; children need a "silent period" with lots of comprehensible input — Krashen, 1982, *clásico*; use it with caution: his hypotheses are influential but debated).
  - Stages: **pre-listening** (activate prior knowledge, pre-teach key words, set a purpose) → **while-listening** (task: point, number, colour, tick) → **post-listening** (use the language).
  - Young learners rely heavily on context and visual support (Cameron, 2001) ([[Cameron_2001_Ninos_Lenguas]]).
- **3.2 Speaking**: progression **imitation → controlled → guided → free production**; chunks and speaking frames; information-gap tasks give a real reason to speak.
  - **Feedback**: prompts that lead pupils to self-correct are more effective than recasts alone (Lyster & Saito, 2010, meta-analysis — ✔ [[Lyster_2010_Feedback_Oral_L2]]).
- **Example (3rd year)**: listening to a weather forecast with symbols → pairs ask and answer *What's the weather like in…?* using a map (information gap) → pupils present the weather for their town.

## 4. Written comprehension and expression
- **4.1 Reading**: written language comes **after** oral familiarity with the words (Cameron, 2001). English is an opaque orthography, so pupils need explicit teaching of sound-spelling patterns (phonics) (see topic 10 — [[Lectoescritura_en_Ingles]]). Reading for pleasure needs very easy texts: around **98 % known words** (Nation, 2013 — ✔ [[Nation_2013_Vocabulario_L2]]).
  - Stages: pre-, while- and post-reading; intensive reading (detailed tasks) and extensive reading (graded readers, class library).
- **4.2 Writing**: **copying → guided writing → free writing**, always with a model; process writing (plan, draft, revise) from the third cycle.
- **Example (5th year)**: pupils read a model postcard, identify its parts, plan their own with a frame and write it for a partner school (eTwinning).

## 5. Communicative competence in English
- **Hymes (1972)** (*clásico*): knowing a language is not only grammar but knowing *what to say, to whom, when and how* — a reaction to Chomsky's purely linguistic competence.
- **Canale & Swain (1980)** (*clásico*): grammatical, sociolinguistic and strategic competence; **Canale (1983)** adds discourse competence. Classroom translation: vocabulary and structures; politeness and register (*please*, *Can I…?*); strategies when language fails (gestures, asking for repetition); connecting sentences into a story.
- **Bachman (1990)** (*clásico*, optional): organisational and pragmatic competence.
- **CEFR (2001, 2020)**: communicative language competences (linguistic, sociolinguistic, pragmatic) + plurilingual and pluricultural competence; Pre-A1 and A1 descriptors for Primary.
- **LOMLOE**: the six specific competences of the FL area are the current, official expression of communicative competence in Primary.

## 6. Attention to diversity and assessment
- UDL: multiple means of representation (visuals, gestures, audio) and of action and expression (oral, drawn, written, recorded) ([[Diseno_Universal_para_el_Aprendizaje]]); Decreto 85/2018.
- Dyslexia: priority to oral skills, no penalty for spelling; newly arrived pupils: visual support and buddy; high ability: extension tasks.
- Assessment of each skill with specific instruments (Orden 185/2022): observation scales for speaking, picture-based listening tasks, rubrics for writing, *can-do* self-assessment ([[Evaluacion_en_Lengua_Extranjera]]).

## 7. Conclusion
- **Model idea**: *"Going back to Lucía and her 'Rain!', developing linguistic skills is not about teaching four separate subjects but about giving children reasons and tools to communicate. When listening, speaking, reading and writing are integrated in meaningful tasks, communicative competence grows naturally. Our challenge as teachers is to create those situations every day."*
