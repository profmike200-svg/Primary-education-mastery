# Topic 7 · Development script

**Title**: Oral language. The complexity of global listening comprehension: from hearing to active and selective understanding. From imitation to free production.

> Guion para redactar el tema. Comprueba antes de citar las referencias marcadas como *clásico*. Ver [[CLM_Como_Escribir_un_Tema_de_10]].

## 1. Introduction
- **Hook (option)**: *"Think of a child listening to a story in English for the first time. At first she only hears sounds; then she catches 'dragon' and 'castle'; by the third reading she shouts the repeated line before the teacher does. In a few days she has travelled from hearing to understanding and from silence to speaking."*
- **Relevance**: oral language is the basis of communication and is pre-eminent in Primary (RD 157/2022 and Decreto 81/2022 give priority to oral comprehension, production and interaction, especially in the first cycle).
- **Outline**.

## 2. Oral language
- Components: **phonological** (sounds, stress, intonation), **lexical**, **grammatical**, **pragmatic/discourse** (turn-taking, register) and **non-verbal** (gestures, facial expression).
- Features of spoken language: spontaneity, redundancy, incomplete sentences, shared context.
- Link to topic 1 (oral pre-eminence) and topic 9 (phonology).

## 3. The complexity of global listening comprehension
- **3.1 Hearing vs listening**: hearing is physical; listening is an active cognitive process of building meaning.
- **3.2 Processing**: **bottom-up** (sounds → words → sentences) and **top-down** (prior knowledge, context, expectations); efficient listeners combine both (Field, 2008; Anderson & Lynch, 1988 — *clásicos*).
- **3.3 From global to selective understanding**: listening for **gist**, for **specific information** and for **detail**; young learners start with global understanding supported by context and visuals (Cameron, 2001 — ✔).
- **3.4 Difficulties**: speed, connected speech, unknown vocabulary, lack of visual support, anxiety. Strategies: repetition, visuals, gestures, pre-teaching, clear purpose.
- **3.5 Metacognitive listening**: teaching pupils to predict, check and evaluate their understanding (Vandergrift & Goh, 2012 — *clásico*) ([[Metacognicion]]).

## 4. Didactic application of listening
- **Pre-listening**: activate knowledge, pre-teach 4-6 key words, set the task.
- **While-listening**: tasks that do not require production: point, number, colour, tick, TPR ([[Respuesta_Fisica_Total_TPR]]).
- **Post-listening**: retell, dramatise, personalise.
- Materials: stories, songs, dialogues, authentic short videos; teacher talk as the main source of input.
- **Example (2nd year)**: story about animals → pupils hold up a card when they hear their animal (selective listening) → sequence pictures (global understanding).

## 5. Speaking: from imitation to free production
- **5.1 Stages**: **imitation** (songs, rhymes, chants, repetition) → **controlled practice** (drills, games with fixed structures) → **guided production** (speaking frames, information gaps, role-plays with cards) → **free production** (presentations, personalised conversations).
- **5.2 Accuracy and fluency** (Brumfit, 1984 — *clásico*): both are needed; in Primary, fluency activities should not be interrupted by constant correction.
- **5.3 Interaction**: interaction and negotiation of meaning support acquisition (Long's interaction hypothesis, 1996); producing language makes learners notice gaps (Swain's output hypothesis, 1985) — *clásicos*.
- **5.4 Corrective feedback**: prompts for self-correction are more effective than recasts alone; younger learners benefit more (Lyster & Saito, 2010 — ✔ [[Lyster_2010_Feedback_Oral_L2]]).

## 6. Didactic application of speaking
- Chunks and classroom language from day one; speaking frames on the wall; pair work before whole-class presentation (lower anxiety).
- **Example (4th year)**: *Find someone who…* survey → pupils report results (*Three children have got a pet*) → short oral presentation of the class graph.

## 7. Attention to diversity and assessment
- UDL; TDL (sentence frames, visuals), TEA (explicit turn-taking rules), shy pupils (recordings), newly arrived pupils (listening before speaking is natural) ([[TDL]], [[TEA]], [[Incorporacion_Tardia]]).
- Assessment: picture-based listening tasks, observation scale for speaking (communication, vocabulary, pronunciation, fluency), recordings to show progress ([[Evaluacion_en_Lengua_Extranjera]]).

## 8. Conclusion
- **Model idea**: *"From the first sounds a child hears to the first sentence she builds on her own, oral language develops step by step. Our role is to provide rich input, real reasons to speak and patient, intelligent feedback, so that every child can travel from imitation to free production."*

## Errores que evitar
- Confundir *hearing* y *listening* o no explicar el paso de global a selectivo (está en el título).
- Presentar la expresión oral como repetición; el título exige llegar a la **producción libre**.
